# LeetCode Premium Alternatives: Cheaper Ways to Cover Company Tags, Editorials and Pattern Practice

Most people searching for LeetCode Premium alternatives aren't shopping for a better platform. They're staring at a $35/month subscription, wondering which parts of it they actually need before their next interview loop, and whether the free versions of those parts are good enough.

Short answer: two of Premium's four headline features have solid free replacements, one doesn't, and one is worth paying for — just not for a full year.

Here's the breakdown, followed by the structured paid option most candidates end up pairing with free LeetCode, and what each plan costs.

## The four things you're actually paying for

Strip out the branding and Premium sells four things.

**Company tags.** Filter problems by what Google, Amazon or Meta asked recently, sorted by frequency and role. This is the single most cited reason people subscribe.

**Official editorials.** Written solutions with multiple approaches and complexity analysis, attached to the problem rather than scattered across the internet.

**The in-browser debugger.** Step through your code against failing test cases without leaving the tab.

**AI features and mock assessment tooling.** Hint generation, and timed simulated interviews.

Now the part that decides whether you need to pay.

| Premium feature | Free replacement | How good is it, honestly |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Official editorials | Community "Solutions" tab, sorted by most voted | Often better. Community write-ups explain why an approach was chosen, not just the mechanics, and you get Python, Java, C++ and JS side by side. Quality is uneven but the top-voted answer usually wins. |
| Editorials, for visual learners | NeetCode's free YouTube walkthroughs | Free for the entire NeetCode 150. Widely treated as the default explanation source in 2026. |
| Structured problem lists | Blind 75, Grind 75, NeetCode 150 | All free. Blind 75 if you have under three weeks; Grind 75 if you want the schedule to adapt to your weekly hours; NeetCode 150 for pattern-first coverage with videos. |
| Company tags | Community-curated GitHub lists, tag aggregators | The weakest link. Free lists are snapshots, not live data, and freshness varies wildly between repos. |

| AI hints | Various third-party extensions and assistants | Crowded category, quality varies. Several are free. |

If your goal is learning the material, that table covers most of it for $0. Two caveats before you cancel anything.

## Where the free route actually breaks

**Company tags are live data, and free lists aren't.** A problem currently circulating in interview loops gets a company-tagged comment on LeetCode within about 48 hours of showing up, according to one platform comparison. A GitHub repo that hasn't been updated in eight months is a historical record. If your onsite is in two weeks, the difference matters.

**Structure costs you time when it's missing.** LeetCode hands you 4,000+ problems and no opinion about order. The standard workaround is to import someone else's list — Blind 75, Grind 75, NeetCode 150 — which works, but leaves you deciding how to sequence topics, when to move on, and how to recognise a pattern you haven't drilled yet. That's the gap paid alternatives are actually selling.

**Nothing free enforces a schedule.** This is the least technical and most common reason people pay for something.

> Worth checking before you buy anything: whether you solve unfamiliar Medium problems without hints. If the answer is no, company tags won't help. Frequency data tells you which problems to prioritise, not how to solve them, and paying for it while you're still building fundamentals puts the cart in front of the horse.

## The structured alternative: teaching patterns instead of selling volume

AlgoMonster takes the opposite position from LeetCode. It's built by ex-Google engineers around 48 coding patterns — reusable templates like sliding window, two pointers, topological sort — with the argument that interviewers test pattern recognition, not whether you've seen 2,000 problems.

The delivery format is text and diagrams, not video: 500+ lessons across 6 courses, 1,000+ illustrations and about 3,000 editorials. Pro also unlocks an interactive code editor, decision flowcharts for working out which pattern a new problem needs, a Speedrun mode for timed drills, unlimited AI assistant usage, and real questions from major tech companies updated monthly.

Two bonus courses come with Pro: a Foundation Course on CS fundamentals, and a Build an LLM course covering tokenisation, attention and training. Some partner-developed courses, such as Quant Interview Prep, are sold separately.

What it doesn't do: it isn't a problem grinder. The mapped problem set is in the low hundreds, not thousands. If your prep style is "volume until it's muscle memory", you'll still want LeetCode's free tier running alongside it. And there are no video walkthroughs, which rules it out if you learn by watching someone think.

## The rest of the market, briefly

**NeetCode Pro** — $119/year, list price $199. The free tier already gives you the Blind 75 and NeetCode 150 roadmaps plus video explanations for every problem; Pro adds progress tracking, spaced repetition, code solutions in seven languages, an expanded problem set and a private Discord. Company-tagged problems across 100+ companies are now listed as an included Pro feature, which is a direct shot at Premium's main selling point. If you already like NeetCode's videos, upgrading is the smallest jump.

**AlgoExpert All Access** — $119 for one year against a $693 list price, and it's the only SKU on the public purchase page. That single price bundles AlgoExpert, SystemsExpert, FrontendExpert, MLExpert, InfraExpert, iOSExpert and BlockchainExpert. 160 curated problems with two-part video explanations and written solutions in nine languages. Narrower problem coverage than LeetCode, far deeper explanations per problem, and it's the only option here with real system design content for senior loops.

**Educative's Grokking series** — the "16 patterns that solve 90% of LeetCode" framing started here. Text-first courses with embedded runnable examples, subscription pricing. Good for people who'd rather read prose than watch a video, but you'll still need a problem grinder afterwards for retention.

**GeeksforGeeks and InterviewBit** — GeeksforGeeks teaches concepts before problems, which is why beginners progress faster there than getting stuck cold on a LeetCode Medium. InterviewBit's value is sequencing: short concept note, then a problem, in a fixed order. Neither is a substitute for a modern interview platform, and GeeksforGeeks is heavily ad-supported on free access.

## How to choose in about two minutes

- **Interview in under three weeks, already comfortable with Mediums:** buy one month of LeetCode Premium, work company-tagged lists, cancel.
- **Six to twelve weeks out, still missing patterns:** AlgoMonster's yearly plan plus free LeetCode for volume. Roughly $99 total, against $159 for a year of Premium alone.
- **You'll interview repeatedly over the next few years:** the $189 lifetime plan, on the same reasoning that makes lifetime deals worth it when you'll come back to the material.
- **You want video explanations everywhere:** NeetCode Pro at $119/year, or NeetCode's free videos plus free LeetCode.
- **Senior loop with a system design round:** AlgoExpert All Access, and budget separately for dedicated system design practice.
- **Budget is genuinely zero:** Blind 75 or Grind 75, the community Solutions tab, NeetCode on YouTube, and Pramp's free mock credits. This gets people hired. It just takes more self-discipline than a paid plan does.

## FAQ

**Is there an AlgoMonster monthly plan?** No. Annual or lifetime only, which is why the $99 yearly plan functions as the low-commitment entry point.

**Does AlgoMonster have videos?** No. The curriculum is text lessons and diagrams. If you need to watch someone solve a problem in real time, that's what NeetCode's free YouTube channel is for — pairing the two is common.

**Can I get LeetCode company tags for free?** Partially. Community GitHub lists and tag aggregators cover the popular problems, and NeetCode Pro now includes company tags across 100+ companies. What you lose is recency, and recency is the whole point of the feature.

**Is the lifetime plan worth $189?** If you'll prep for interviews more than once — promotions, job changes, a return to the market — yes, on simple arithmetic. If this is a one-off search and you'll never open the platform again, the yearly plan or the free tier covers you.

**Do I need AlgoMonster and LeetCode Premium together?** No. AlgoMonster's mapped problems cover the pattern work. Adding Premium during a final sprint for company-specific frequency data is a legitimate combo, but it's a two-week purchase, not a twelve-month one.
